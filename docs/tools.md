# Tool reference

dogpolicies-mcp 0.5.0, generated by `node scripts/docs.mjs` from the live definitions. Every tool returns readable markdown by default and structured JSON with `response_format: "json"`; every result starts with a citation and ends with the attribution line.

## `dogpolicies_search_places`

**Search places** · since 0.1.0

Can my dog go to this place, or which parks, beaches, trails, campgrounds or patios allow dogs: "Can I bring my dog to Point Lobos?", "state parks in Marin that allow dogs", "off-leash beaches near San Diego"?
Answers for one named place or a filtered list of one kind; each result's id names the place's full record, with every rule and source.
Inputs: query (name, city or brand; words like "cabins", "hikes", "wineries" or "dog friendly hotels in Carmel" are read as the kind and the place), type (lodging, campground incl. RV parks, cabins, beach, park, trail, restaurant, rest_area, stop; "hotel", "dog park" or "hike" are read as these), dogs_allowed, off_leash, state "CA" or "NV", county without "County", area (lake-tahoe, big-sur, east-bay), or near={lat, lon}.
Each result: yes, yes_restricted, no or varies, the rule in a sentence, verified date, URL and id. No match is an answer (outcome no_match) with did_you_mean, nearest and next. Nevada coverage is thin.

Examples:
- "Off-leash beaches near San Diego" -> type="beach", off_leash=true, near={lat:32.72, lon:-117.16}
- "Can I bring my dog to Point Lobos?" -> query="Point Lobos"
- "State parks in Marin County that allow dogs" -> type="park", county="Marin", dogs_allowed=true
- "Lake Tahoe beaches in Nevada that allow dogs" -> type="beach", state="NV", dogs_allowed=true
- "Off-leash dog parks in Las Vegas" -> type="park", off_leash=true, county="Clark", state="NV"
- "RV parks in Fresno County that take dogs" -> type="campground", county="Fresno", dogs_allowed=true
- "Dog-friendly cabins in June Lake" -> type="cabins", query="June Lake", dogs_allowed=true (query="June Lake cabins" works too)
- "Restaurants with a dog-friendly patio in Alameda" -> type="restaurant", query="Alameda"

Annotations: read-only true, destructive false, idempotent true, open-world false.

| Input | Type | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `query` | string (min length 2, max length 100) | Words to match in the name, city, county or brand |
| `type` | `lodging`, `campground`, `cabins`, `beach`, `park`, `trail`, `restaurant`, `rest_area`, `stop` | Kind of place; campground covers campgrounds and RV parks (some also rent cabins), cabins covers cabin resorts with no campground, restaurant covers restaurants, cafes, bars, breweries and tasting rooms (dogs on the patio, by the owner's published policy), rest_area and stop are the roadside kinds |
| `dogs_allowed` | boolean | true: only places that allow dogs somewhere; false: only places that do not |
| `off_leash` | boolean | true: only places where the managing agency allows dogs off leash, at least at some times |
| `state` | `CA`, `NV` | "CA" or "NV". Also tells apart counties with the same name, such as Humboldt. |
| `county` | string (min length 2, max length 40) | County name without "County", in California or Nevada, e.g. "Monterey" or "Washoe" |
| `region` | `north-coast`, `shasta-cascade`, `gold-country`, `high-sierra`, `bay-area`, `central-coast`, `central-valley`, `inland-empire-deserts`, `los-angeles`, `orange-county`, `san-diego`, `reno-tahoe-nevada`, `las-vegas-southern-nevada`, `northern-central-nevada` | Region id, e.g. central-coast, san-diego or reno-tahoe-nevada |
| `area` | string (min length 2, max length 60) | Area id, the name a traveler gives the trip: san-francisco, east-bay, peninsula, south-bay, north-bay, lake-tahoe, big-sur, mendocino-coast, yosemite, death-valley |
| `near` | object | Limit to places near a point; results are sorted by distance |
| `limit` | integer (min 1, max 50, default `10`) | Maximum results to return |
| `offset` | integer (min 0, max 9007199254740991, default `0`) | Results to skip, for pagination |
| `response_format` | `markdown`, `json` (default `"markdown"`) | 'markdown' for readable text, 'json' for structured data |

Output fields: `as_of`, `license`, `attribution_text`, `attribution_url`, `outcome`, `code`, `message`, `did_you_mean`, `nearest`, `next`, `accepted`, `resolved_from`, `total`, `count`, `offset`, `has_more`, `places` (more may be added; none removed).

## `dogpolicies_near`

**What is near a town** · since 0.2.0

What can my dog do in or around this town, neighborhood or region: beaches, parks, trails, hotels, campgrounds, cabins, patios and rest areas, nearest first?
Answers "dog-friendly things in X" questions, grouped by kind. It does not check hotels against a dog's weight or a fee budget.
Input: place as people say it: a city, county, neighborhood or alias ("Van Nuys", "Koreatown", "Venice Beach", "the Mission", "DTLA"), an area or region ("Lake Tahoe", "East Bay", "Central Valley"), or near={lat, lon}. Small spelling slips ("Montery") are corrected and labeled. If nothing is inside the radius it widens to 30, 60, then 100 miles and says so.
Each result carries the dog rule, verified date and source URL. An unknown name comes back as an answer with did_you_mean, the nearest towns and next (a larger town or county that is covered).

Examples:
- "What can my dog do in Carmel?" -> place="Carmel"
- "Dog beaches and parks around South Lake Tahoe" -> place="South Lake Tahoe", types=["beach","park"]
- "Anything dog-friendly in Van Nuys?" -> place="Van Nuys"
- "Anything dog-friendly within 5 miles of here" -> near={lat:36.6, lon:-121.9}, radius_miles=5

Annotations: read-only true, destructive false, idempotent true, open-world false.

| Input | Type | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `place` | string (min length 2, max length 80) | A city, county, area, region, neighborhood or airport in California or Nevada, e.g. "Monterey", "South Lake Tahoe", "Washoe County, NV", "Las Vegas", "East Bay", "Lake Tahoe", "Central Valley", "Van Nuys", "Koreatown", "Venice Beach", "the Mission", "DTLA", "SFO" or "LAX airport" (an airport searches 5 miles unless radius_miles is given). Accepts the place as people say it; small spelling slips ("Montery") are corrected and the answer says so. |
| `near` | object | A point as an alternative to a town: one of place or near |
| `radius_miles` | number (min 1, max 100, default `15`) | Search radius in miles around the town or point (an area is its own boundary; the radius is ignored) |
| `types` | array of `beach`, `park`, `trail`, `lodging`, `campground`, `cabins`, `restaurant`, `rest_area`, `stop` | Kinds to include; all by default |
| `dogs_allowed_only` | boolean (default `true`) | Leave out places where dogs are not allowed |
| `per_type` | integer (min 1, max 20, default `5`) | Nearest results to show per kind |
| `response_format` | `markdown`, `json` (default `"markdown"`) | 'markdown' for readable text, 'json' for structured data |

Output fields: `as_of`, `license`, `attribution_text`, `attribution_url`, `outcome`, `code`, `message`, `did_you_mean`, `nearest`, `next`, `accepted`, `resolved_from`, `center`, `label`, `radius_miles`, `radius_widened_from`, `total`, `groups` (more may be added; none removed).

## `dogpolicies_get_place`

**Get place details** · since 0.1.0

What are the exact dog rules for this one place, and what is the official source: the quote, the publisher, the date checked?
The leash rule, where dogs may go, fees and limits in full, with the official source and its quote, for one place known by id or name.
Input: id as returned in any result (for example "carmel-beach"); an exact name works, and a near miss resolves to the one clear match, labeled resolved_from. An id nothing matches is an answer with did_you_mean, not an error.
Returns every rule with display text and rule ids, each source with URL, publisher, capture date and quote, and facts the source does not state listed as not_stated. A place on I-5 in California also carries its position on the road (Caltrans mile, side, distance off the freeway) and any paired exit.

Annotations: read-only true, destructive false, idempotent true, open-world false.

| Input | Type | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `id` (required) | string (min length 2, max length 120) | Place id, e.g. "fort-funston" |
| `response_format` | `markdown`, `json` (default `"markdown"`) | 'markdown' for readable text, 'json' for structured data |

Output fields: `id`, `uid`, `name`, `type`, `status`, `answer`, `today`, `citation`, `confidence`, `confirmed_by`, `freshness`, `verified_at`, `url`, `source_urls`, `record_license`, `closed_now`, `as_of`, `license`, `attribution_text`, `attribution_url`, `outcome`, `code`, `message`, `did_you_mean`, `nearest`, `next`, `accepted`, `resolved_from`, `facts`, `rule_records`, `not_stated`, `rules`, `sources`, `review_due`, `axis`, `paired_stop` (more may be added; none removed).

## `dogpolicies_compare_places`

**Compare places side by side** · since 0.2.0

Which of these places is better for my dog: Carmel Beach or Asilomar, two parks, three hotels?
Sets two to six known places side by side, fact by fact.
Input: ids from search results ("carmel-beach", "asilomar-state-beach"); an exact name or a near miss resolves to the one clear match.
Returns one row per fact (dogs allowed, leash rule, where dogs may go, pet fee, weight limit, pets per room, restrictions) and one column per place, with confidence, freshness, who confirmed it, and a citation for each. An id that matches nothing is listed under missing with close matches while the rest are still compared (outcome partial); fewer than two found is no_match with suggestions.

Annotations: read-only true, destructive false, idempotent true, open-world false.

| Input | Type | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `ids` (required) | array of string | Two to six place ids from search results |
| `response_format` | `markdown`, `json` (default `"markdown"`) | 'markdown' for readable text, 'json' for structured data |

Output fields: `as_of`, `license`, `attribution_text`, `attribution_url`, `outcome`, `code`, `message`, `did_you_mean`, `nearest`, `next`, `accepted`, `resolved_from`, `places`, `rows`, `missing` (more may be added; none removed).

## `dogpolicies_plan_trip`

**Plan a trip with a dog** · since 0.2.0

Plan a dog trip to a town: where can we stay with this dog, what is the pet fee for all the nights, what can we do each day, where can we eat?
One answer covers a whole stay, such as "three nights in Monterey with our 70 lb Lab, pet fees under $150" or "camping near Mammoth Lakes".
Inputs: place (a city, county, neighborhood, area or region, with small spelling slips corrected) or near={lat, lon}; dog_weight_lb, dogs, nights, max_total_fee, stay_types. Optional: start_date (dates each day), stay_id (build the plan on that stay), save=true (adds trip_url, a dogpolicies.com page showing the plan; the plan travels inside the link, nothing is stored).
Returns stays that fit with the whole-stay pet fee worked out (a pet fee, never a room rate), stays that need a call to confirm, and "days": for each night the stay, a morning beach, trail or off-leash area, an afternoon park and a dinner patio, each a real record with source URL and verified date; a slot the data cannot fill is null with the reason (dinner often is). "plan_stay" shows the fee arithmetic and whether the dog's weight and number fit. Within 10 miles of I-5 in California it adds the drive leg: the town's mile and the next family stops each way. An unknown town is an answer with did_you_mean. The markdown answer is laid out as a trip document for the traveler.

Examples:
- "Three nights in Monterey with our 70 lb Lab, pet fees under $100" -> place="Monterey", dog_weight_lb=70, nights=3, max_total_fee=100
- "A weekend in South Lake Tahoe with two small dogs" -> place="South Lake Tahoe", dogs=2, nights=2
- "Camping near Mammoth Lakes with a dog" -> place="Mono County", stay_types=["campground","cabins"]
- "Plan 4 nights in Carmel with two dogs and give me a link" -> place="Carmel-by-the-Sea", dogs=2, nights=4, save=true

Annotations: read-only true, destructive false, idempotent true, open-world false.

| Input | Type | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `place` | string (min length 2, max length 80) | A city, county, area, region, neighborhood or airport in California or Nevada, e.g. "Monterey", "South Lake Tahoe", "Washoe County, NV", "Las Vegas", "East Bay", "Lake Tahoe", "Central Valley", "Van Nuys", "Koreatown", "Venice Beach", "the Mission", "DTLA", "SFO" or "LAX airport" (an airport searches 5 miles unless radius_miles is given). Accepts the place as people say it; small spelling slips ("Montery") are corrected and the answer says so. |
| `near` | object | A point as an alternative to a town: one of place or near |
| `radius_miles` | number (min 1, max 100, default `15`) | Search radius in miles around the town or point (an area is its own boundary; the radius is ignored) |
| `dog_weight_lb` | number (min 1, max 300) | The dog's weight in pounds |
| `dogs` | integer (min 1, max 10, default `1`) | Number of dogs |
| `nights` | integer (min 1, max 90, default `1`) | Nights of the stay |
| `max_total_fee` | number (min 0, max 10000) | Most the guest will pay in pet fees for the whole stay, USD |
| `stay_types` | array of `lodging`, `campground`, `cabins` (default `["lodging","campground","cabins"]`) | Kinds of stay to consider |
| `include_possible` | boolean (default `true`) | Include stays that need a call to confirm |
| `per_section` | integer (min 1, max 15, default `5`) | Results per section |
| `start_date` | string | First night, YYYY-MM-DD; each day of the plan then carries its date |
| `stay_id` | string (min length 2, max length 140) | The stay the day-by-day plan is built on (an id from stays); the first stay by default |
| `save` | boolean (default `false`) | Also return trip_url: a link to this plan on dogpolicies.com, with the plan in the link itself. Nothing is stored |
| `response_format` | `markdown`, `json` (default `"markdown"`) | 'markdown' for readable text, 'json' for structured data |

Output fields: `as_of`, `license`, `attribution_text`, `attribution_url`, `outcome`, `code`, `message`, `did_you_mean`, `nearest`, `next`, `accepted`, `resolved_from`, `label`, `radius_miles`, `asked`, `stays`, `stay_counts`, `days`, `plan_stay`, `plan_notes`, `trip_url`, `outings`, `i5` (more may be added; none removed).

## `dogpolicies_find_hotels`

**Find hotels for a dog** · since 0.1.0

Will a hotel take my dog, and what will the pet fee come to: a 70 lb Lab under $50, two dogs for three nights near Palm Springs, the Motel 6 in Fresno?
Does the weight, dog-count and fee arithmetic for the stay, hotel by hotel, against each hotel's published pet policy.
Inputs: query (hotel name, city or brand), brand, county without "County", state "CA" or "NV", area (lake-tahoe, big-sur, east-bay), region, or near={lat, lon}; dog_weight_lb, dogs, nights, max_total_fee.
Each hotel is a match, a possible (needs a call: case by case, weight unstated, "up to" fee) or excluded, with the arithmetic spelled out, the source URL and verified date. Amounts are pet fees, never room rates: there are no room prices here.

Examples:
- "Will a Monterey hotel take my 70 lb Lab for under $50?" -> county="Monterey", dog_weight_lb=70, max_total_fee=50
- "Two dogs, three nights near Palm Springs" -> near={lat:33.83, lon:-116.55}, dogs=2, nights=3
- "Is the Motel 6 in Fresno dog friendly?" -> query="Motel 6 Fresno"
- "A hotel near LAX for my dog" -> query="LAX" (an airport code or name searches 5 miles around the airport)
- "A Las Vegas hotel for my 60 lb dog" -> county="Clark", state="NV", dog_weight_lb=60

Annotations: read-only true, destructive false, idempotent true, open-world false.

## `dogpolicies_route_stops`

**Dog stops along a highway** · since 0.1.0

Where can we stop with the dog along this highway, in driving order: rest areas, travel stops with dog parks, hotels, campgrounds, parks and beaches, each with its mile and distance off the road?
For a drive on I-5, US-101, Highway 1, I-15, I-80 or US-50 in California, or I-15, I-80, US-50, US-95 or US-93 in Nevada (each route stops at the state line). It lists stops, not closures or chain controls.
Inputs: route (default i-5), a start as near={lat, lon} or from_mile, direction (north, south, east, west), ahead_miles or to_mile, dog_weight_lb and max_total_fee for stays. On I-5 add family=true (restrooms and a pet area stated by the source) or paired=true (an exit with places for people, plus the nearest dog-legal place).
Each stop has its mile, side, distance off the road, dog rule, source URL and verified date; stays carry the whole-stay pet fee. On I-5 a rest area Caltrans reports fully closed right now carries closed_now (Caltrans' reason and end time), is left out of the "first open stop" in the text, and stays in the list.

Examples:
- "Driving north on I-5 from Los Angeles with a 70 lb dog, where can we stop?" -> near={lat:34.05, lon:-118.24}, direction="north", dog_weight_lb=70
- "Pet areas on the Grapevine" -> from_mile=170, to_mile=222, kinds=["rest_area","park"]
- "Next restroom stop for the whole family, dog included, 40 miles north of Harris Ranch" -> near={lat:36.25, lon:-120.25}, direction="north", ahead_miles=40, family=true
- "Heading south on 101 from San Francisco, where can the dog run?" -> route="us-101", near={lat:37.77, lon:-122.42}, direction="south"
- "Driving I-80 east from Reno with a dog" -> route="i-80-nevada", near={lat:39.53, lon:-119.81}, direction="east"

Annotations: read-only true, destructive false, idempotent true, open-world false.

## `dogpolicies_i5_corridor`

**I-5 corridor by stretch** · since 0.3.0

What is on I-5 between here and there for a dog: the next restroom stops with a pet area, the stops on the Grapevine, between Sacramento and Redding?
For the freeway in California only, by one of eleven named stretches (sandiego, orange-la, grapevine, kern-westside, westside, delta, sacramento, valley, tehama, shasta, siskiyou), a mile range, or a window ahead of a point. It covers I-5 only, with no hotel fee arithmetic and no closures or chain controls.
